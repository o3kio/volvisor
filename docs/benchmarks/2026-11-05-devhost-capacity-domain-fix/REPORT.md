## 1. Scope and what changed

The slice-2 grow-lock fix (`8029df8`) released the provider state lock across
`lvextend`, but its re-measurement left the c128 write p95 at
**3,413.886 ms**, above the older `aab773b` baseline (1,552.261 ms). A
grows-disabled diagnostic attributed the residual to the grow path, not to
attach/report/detach.

Three commits follow:

- **`a3ea5abf` — allocation-epoch grow admission.** Capacity is sampled
  outside the provider state lock; a per-VG in-memory reservation is then
  taken under the state lock; an allocation epoch invalidates a sample that
  raced with any allocation; the durable grow intent is published under the
  state lock; a bounded optimistic-retry cap turns sustained churn into a
  typed refusal instead of a loop. Grow drives are guarded per volume and run
  through `run_blocking_body`.
- **`6519068` — drop the grow-drive bulkhead.** A same-commit A/B diagnostic
  (limit 8 versus effectively unbounded at c128) measured the bounded arm at
  lower c128 throughput and longer grow latency while the LVM-bound grow
  completion rate stayed roughly constant. The provider-level semaphore was
  removed; in-flight grows are bounded only by the daemon's request
  admission cap (256 in this run). The per-volume drive guard and
  `run_blocking_body` remain.
- **`4290b84` — review fixes.** Two Medium defects found by adversarial
  review (below) were fixed before this measurement. This commit is the
  measured binary and source; the first two commits are part of the same
  measured tree and were not separately published as a bundle.

The earlier clean run at `6519068` was invalidated by `4290b84` and is
**not** evidence for this tree. Its numbers are not used here.

## 2. Diagnosis that preceded the fix

The earlier diagnostic and the `8029df8` re-measurement left two questions:
why a c128 write tail remained, and whether the grow admission itself
convoyed. The design read is:

- The capacity sample (`vgs`) was serialized behind a capacity-domain
  mutex. Concurrent grows therefore lined up on the sample, and a create
  could spend capacity between a grow's sample and its reservation. The
  epoch protocol moves the sample outside the state lock and revalidates it
  before reserving.
- The grow itself is LVM-per-VG-bound. `vgs`, `lvextend` and the
  size-verifying `lvs` serialize on the host's LVM lock. No provider-side
  change can make 128 simultaneous resizes complete faster than that
  serialization allows. The measured c128 grow completion rate in this run
  is **11.118/s** (438 grows over 39.394 s).

The bulkhead A/B is a **non-evidence diagnostic** at the parent commit
`a3ea5abf`: both arms were built from the same tree with only
`GROW_CONCURRENCY_LIMIT` varied. It is not a published bundle and is not
used here as a product measurement. Its purpose was to decide whether to
keep the semaphore; the answer was no. The diagnostic does not attribute
the c128 grow tail beyond the provider-side queue it added.

## 3. Adversarial review and the two Medium fixes

Two independent read-only reviews covered the epoch/publish protocol, the
bulkhead removal, the admission coupling, benchmark validity and the residual
list. Neither found a Critical or High runtime-correctness defect. They found
two Medium defects, both fixed at `4290b84` before this run:

- **Create-attempt epoch window.** A create whose `lvcreate` outcome was
  unknown, exited non-zero, failed size verification, or was adopted on retry
  could spend capacity without bumping the allocation epoch, so a grow that
  sampled before the attempt could reserve against the pre-attempt figure.
  The fix bumps the epoch on every post-`lvcreate` exit path and whenever a
  durable create intent is found on retry. It is conservative on an attempt
  that never reached LVM (one extra capacity sample). Three integration tests
  cover the unknown outcome, the known failed start, and adoption; all three
  fail on the unfixed parent as negative controls.
- **Publish error path.** The grow publish discarded
  `lock_state_stable()`'s `Result`, so on a poisoned state mutex or an
  unreadable alignment load it bumped the epoch and released the reservation
  without the state guard and with no observable error. The fix matches the
  result, keeps the bump and drop on both paths, and emits a structured
  warning when the lock cannot be held. The unlocked drop is safe today
  because a create holds the state mutex across its sample and reservation
  read, and a poisoned mutex also refuses new creates; the warning keeps the
  degraded path visible.

The reviews also corrected the record:

- The `a3ea5abf` commit message claimed the retry cap was covered by
  negative controls. The cap **is** exercised by a unit test
  (`sustained_epoch_churn_refuses_after_the_attempt_cap`), but it was not
  part of the negative-control set. The provider-level `OVERLOADED` is raised
  after the journal intent and is therefore the **terminal** outcome for that
  `operation_id`; a retry needs a new id. The constant, test and error detail
  now say so.
- "Bounded by the admission cap" is a **count** bound only. The cap is
  shared with reads and attaches, mutations have no execution timeout, and
  rate limiting defaults to off. A sustained grow flood can shed
  reads/attaches with `503` after the queue timeout, and
  `max_concurrent_requests` can be raised to 65536 without an LVM
  cross-check. This is documented in `docs/admission-control.md`; the
  semaphore is not re-added, because the A/B showed it did not raise the
  LVM-bound completion rate.
- The c128 aggregate write p95 is a quantile of a bimodal mixture sitting
  near the 5 % grow-share boundary. It flips between the non-grow and grow
  populations and must not be compared head-to-head across SHAs. §11
  publishes the per-operation split, realized grow share, grow completion
  rate and same-SHA repeats.

Static checks at `4290b84`: `cargo fmt --all --check`,
`cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --locked -- -D warnings`, full
`cargo test --workspace --locked`, and
`RUSTDOCFLAGS="-D warnings" cargo doc --workspace --no-deps --locked` pass.
The three new create/epoch negative controls fail at the unfixed parent.

### 11.2 What changed in the workload

The candidate's non-grow path is much faster, so its workers complete more
cycles and offer more grows in the same 30 s:

- total ops **13,317 → 25,816 (+93.9%)**; writes 3,861 → 7,544 (+95.4%);
  reads 9,456 → 18,272 (+93.2%).
- grow completions **255 → 438 (+71.8%)**; grow completion rate
  **8.433/s → 11.118/s (+31.9%)**.
- non-grow write p95: attach 3,200.094 → 211.323 ms (15.1× lower), report
  3,572.972 → 194.448 ms (18.4×), detach 3,642.250 → 205.577 ms (17.7×).
- c128 read p95 87.276 → 6.584 ms (13.3× lower).

The grow p50 rise (338.017 → 8,338.505 ms) is queueing, not a slower
per-request path: interval reconstruction from the recorded operation
start/end times shows the candidate's c128 burst reaches the full client
width (128 concurrent grow requests; 8029df8: 6), while the LVM-bound
completion rate rises. The candidate's 438 grows completed in 39.394 s; the
comparator's 255 completed in 30.24 s. The same burst effect is visible at
c8/c32 (grow p50 280.104 → 569.102 ms and 292.593 → 2,358.653 ms), but grows
are only 0.092 % and 0.541 % of writes there, so the c8/c32 write p95
improvements are non-grow improvements, not grow improvements.

### 11.4 What this measurement supports

- The capacity-sample convoy and the state-lock grow serialization are
  gone: c32/c128 non-grow write and read tails fall sharply, and throughput
  rises at every tier and in the soak.
- Capacity atomicity is preserved by the epoch/reservation protocol; the
  crash-recovery anchor remains the durable grow intent. These are
  test-covered at the measured SHA, not measured by this harness.

It does **not** support: a claim that the c128 grow tail is fixed; a claim
that the aggregate c128 write p95 improved; a blanket throughput-gain
claim; a capacity, SLA, durability or data-path claim; or the 24-hour soak.

## 12. Caveats and NOT RUN

- Single host, loop-backed LVM, loopback HTTP, control plane only. Not a
  data-path benchmark, not production capacity, not an SLA, not a
  durability test.
- The 24-hour soak is **NOT RUN**; the run is 25 minutes with one restart.
- No second physical host, real Ceph, DRBD, physical-disk LVM, witness or
  storage-side fencing. Multi-host loss and D3 storage-side fencing are not
  exercised.
- No crash-consistency fault injection in this harness; the generation
  fence and crash semantics are covered by the test suite, not this run.
- Single non-fixed-dataset runs; one sample per tier. The c128 aggregate
  p95 is composition- and run-sensitive (§11.3).
- RSS and journal are single-run observations. RSS peak is 2,361,868 KiB;
  a leak is neither proven nor ruled out. The default INFO daemon log is
  unbounded (2.24 GiB raw here), an operational finding.
- The cap-32 and rate-50 probes are diagnostic shapes, not capacity
  results.
- Residuals, honestly recorded: the c128 grow queue/tail is unresolved and
  LVM-per-VG-bound; shared-cap grow starvation is documented but not solved;
  create still holds the state lock across its `vgs` sample and
  `lvcreate`/`lvs`; cumulative-run RSS/journal effects are not attributed;
  `block_in_place` worker-set cost is not measured; move admission remains
  outside the epoch protocol; retry `resume_pending_grow` and
  `settle_failed_lvextend` run `lvs` under the state lock; in-doubt journal
  intents accumulate across restarts; duplicate-grow refusals are journaled
  terminal failures; the INFO log is unbounded.
- #62 stays open. No gate verdict changes. No production-support, SLA,
  zero-RPO, release or hardware/multi-host claim is made.
